## 4. Specific Transformation Rules Extracted

### 4.1 Vowel Digraph Reduction/Substitution (Highest frequency)

This is the writer's most consistent pattern. Vowel digraphs are reduced to a single vowel based on phonetic approximation.

| Digraph | Transformation | Examples | Count | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ea | ea → e | screamed→skremed, screaming→skreming | 2 | High (consistent across instances) |
| ai | ai → e | fainted→fented | 1 | Medium |
| ai | ai → a | again→agan | 1 | Medium |
| ei | ei → i | heights→hights | 2 | High (consistent across instances) |
| ear | ear → ere | heard→hered | 1 | Medium |
| ew | ew → aw | flew→flaw | 1 | Medium |
| ee | ee → y | bungee→bugy | 1 | Medium |

**Inferred rule:** The writer consistently reduces vowel digraphs to a single phonetically-close vowel. This should generalize to other digraphs (oa→o, ou→ow, etc.) with medium confidence.

### 4.2 Consonant Cluster/Digraph Substitution

| Pattern | Transformation | Examples | Count | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sc (initial) | sc → sk | screamed→skremed, screaming→skreming | 2 | High |
| gr (initial) | gr → g | grabbed→gab | 1 | Medium |
| ng (medial) | ng → g | bungee→bugy, jumping→juming | 2 | High |
| mp (final) | mp → m | jumping→juming | 1 | Medium |
| c (in multisyllable) | c → k | balcony→belkany | 1 | Medium |

**Inferred rule:** Initial and medial consonant clusters are simplified, typically by dropping one consonant (usually the liquid r/l or the nasal).

### 4.3 Multi-Syllable Word Phonetic Restructuring

Complex words are respelled with significant phonetic approximation, often altering vowels across multiple syllables.

| Written | Target | Syllable Changes |
|---|---|---|
| belkany | balcony | bal→bel, co→ka, ny→ny |
| bailden | building | build→bail, -ding→-den |
| bugy-juming | bungee jumping | bun→bu, -gee→-gy, jump→jum, -ing→-ing |
| Bowring | boring | bor→Bow, -ing→-ring |
| selen | ceiling | ceil→sel, -ing→-en |

**Pattern:** Multi-syllable words are the most error-prone. The writer preserves approximate phonetic contour but restructures internal vowels and sometimes consonants.

### 4.4 Morphological: Irregular Past Tense Errors

| Written | Target | Type |
|---|---|---|
| falled | fell | Over-regularization (adding -ed to base form) |
| see | saw | Base/present form used for past |

**Pattern:** Irregular past tenses are either over-regularized (fall→falled) or left in base form (fall, see). This is attested but overlaps with SLI and younger typically-developing writer patterns (see refinements doc §5.2).

## 9. Recommended Rule Engine Priorities

Based on frequency and confidence from this profile:

### Tier 1: Use freely (high frequency, confirmed consistency)
- Vowel digraph reduction (ea→e, ei→i)
- Consonant digraph substitution (sc→sk)
- Multi-syllable phonetic restructuring
- Nasal cluster simplification (ng→g)

### Tier 2: Use with caution (moderate frequency or low sample size)
- Other vowel digraph reductions (ai→a/e, ew→aw, ear→ere)
- Irregular past tense errors
- Missing inflectional suffixes
- Initial consonant cluster reduction

### Tier 3: Use sparingly or label distinctly
- R-intrusion (bark→back) — single instance, uncertain provenance
- Letter confusion (M→N) — may be handwriting-specific
- Apostrophe omission — common in all children's writing, not dyslexia-specific
